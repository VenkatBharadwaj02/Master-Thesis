# Abstract

Finding modern techniques that bypass the need for Feynman rules has played a crucial role in simplifying the complex calculations involved in computing scattering amplitudes at higher orders and/or high multiplicities. 6D supersymmetric theories have a rich structure while admitting dimensional reduction to known 4D theories, making them a natural setting to study scattering amplitudes. In this thesis, we examine techniques for constructing tree-level scattering amplitudes in supersymmetric 6D theories. We start by reviewing spinor helicity, on-shell recursion relations, and supersymmetry in 6D. Using known expressions from 6D and 5D supersymmetric theories, we construct the 3,4-point superamplitudes in the $\mathcal{N}$=(1,0) super quantum chromodynamics theory. As an alternative to on-shell recursion relations, we introduce a bootstrap approach that uses supersymmetric constraints and amplitude relations, which is then used to construct a 5-point amplitude in the SQCD theory. Using the double copy structure, the corresponding 3,4,5-point superamplitudes are constructed in the $\mathcal{N}$=(2,0) chiral and $\mathcal{N}$=(1,1) non-chiral supergravity theories. Finally, we also provide explicit maps from the 6D theories to 4D $\mathcal{N}=2$ SQCD and $\mathcal{N}=4$ supergravity theories that can be used to construct massive superamplitudes in 4D from the 6D amplitudes.
